● Round 2 · Gem & Path

Refining the two finalists.

Five variations of Gem and five of Path, each pushing a different lever — symmetry, weight, negative space, level of abstraction. The goal: find the one mark that survives everywhere and feels unmistakably Questify.

Gem family G1 → G5

The gem says reward, achievement, milestone. The risk is looking generic or RPG-cheesy — these variations dial that back with precise geometry, asymmetry, and clean negative space.

G1 · Original

Classic facet

The starting point — symmetric diamond with two facets.

● G2 · Recommended

Cut gem

Solid silhouette with two cut facets giving depth without strokes. Reads as one shape at 16px.

G3

Q-gem

A diamond with a small tail that turns it into a hidden "Q". Subtle and ownable.

G4

Stacked rewards

Three gems gaining size — the gem becomes a level-up timeline.

G5

Half-cut

Asymmetric — half outline, half solid. Distinctive but slightly tricky at small sizes.

G6

Crystal core

A gem cradled inside a circle — sealed, precious, tokenized. Works beautifully as an app icon.

Path family P1 → P5

Path says journey, step-by-step, progression. The risk is looking like a generic chart or a stair icon — these variations push toward something more architectural, more intentional.

P1 · Original

Stairs + flag

Steps up, sealed by a solid block at the top. Clear but reads as "chart".

● P2 · Recommended

The Trail

One single fluid curve from start dot to goal dot. Soft, optimistic, and unmistakable at any size.

P3

Tiered

Four terraces stacking toward a peak. The gradient of opacity reads as building toward.

P4

Waypoints

Two checkpoints leading to a star — overt, story-driven. Risks looking like a treasure-map cliché.

P5

Open Q

A single-stroke Q that doesn't quite close — a path you're still walking. Clever but reads as a letter first.

P6

Ridge

A polyline of ascending peaks. Active and direct, but flirts with the sport-tracker cliché.

P2 — The Trail

If I had to pick just one across both families, this is it. A single curve that reads as a path, an arc of progress, and (squint) a quiet lowercase q. It's the only mark in either family that carries motion — your eye walks it from the small start dot to the larger goal dot.

  • One continuous stroke. Trivial to redraw, animate, scale.
  • Asymmetric weight (small dot → big dot) communicates direction without an arrow.
  • Pairs with the Geist wordmark beautifully — same round terminals.
  • Survives at 16px because the silhouette is unique, not detail-dependent.
