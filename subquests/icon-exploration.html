A new mark for Questify.

The current compass + mountain reads as outdoor / hiking app — too literal, too crowded a metaphor space (Strava, AllTrails, Komoot). These six concepts keep the spirit of direction and progress but speak the language of a modern productivity tool: geometric, monochrome, scalable to 16px.

Concepts 01 → 06
Concept 01

Path

A staircase climbing from base to summit, sealed by a solid block — the journey's last step. The cleanest expression of "progress" without a single literal element.

PathProgressGeometric
Concept 02

Marker

A quest marker — half RPG waypoint, half map pin. Calm and centered. Reads instantly as "place to reach" and is the most playful of the six without slipping into game-tropes.

Concept 03

Ascent

Three stacked chevrons gaining weight as they rise — XP gained, level reached. Echoes the rank insignia metaphor that gamers already understand.

LevelsXPInsignia
Concept 04

Gem

A faceted gem — the reward at the end of a quest. Most ornamental of the set, leans into the RPG side. Works beautifully at 64px+, less so at 16.

Concept 05

North Star

A four-point star — the metaphor of guidance stripped of any compass-rose decoration. The closest replacement for the current logo while shedding the literal hiking association.

● Concept 06 · Recommended

Threshold

An arch over a horizon, with a single dot stepping through. Reads as a doorway, a portal, a level gate — the moment of crossing into the next chapter. It carries the same emotion as "summit" but is architectural, not outdoorsy.

JourneyThresholdArchitectural

Why "Threshold" wins

It's the only concept that says "crossing into something new" instead of "climbing toward something far away" — which matches Questify better. You're not asking users to reach a distant peak; you're asking them to step through one quest at a time.

  • Reads as a single shape at 16×16 — the dot survives, the arch survives, no lost detail.
  • The dot is a built-in "progress marker" you can animate (slide left → right when XP is earned).
  • Geometric primitives only (arc + line + circle) — easy to redraw in any tool, any weight.
  • Doesn't conflict with Strava's chevron, AllTrails' tree, or Notion's letter mark.
